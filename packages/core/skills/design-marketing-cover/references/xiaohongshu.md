# Xiaohongshu cover reference

Use this reference for Xiaohongshu note covers. Treat it as a practical creative heuristic, not a guarantee about distribution.

## Format and roles

- Default a portrait note cover to 3:4, commonly 1080×1440, unless the user's publishing workflow requires another size.
- Treat the image as the stopping device and the overlay copy as the clicking reason.
- Design for a two-column discovery feed and test the cover at small size.
- Make the cover and note title complementary. The title can carry topic/search context while the cover carries self-relevance, payoff, or tension.

## Strong focal image options

1. A high-contrast scene with a clear social or visual tension.
2. A genuine emotional close-up relevant to the content.
3. A visible, source-supported outcome or comparison.
4. A first-person view that puts the reader inside the task.

Prefer self-insertion over generic prettiness. The viewer should recognize a situation they want, fear, or already experience.

## Overlay copy

Check three questions:

- Does the target reader know it concerns them?
- Is the gain or avoided mistake visible?
- Is there a credible reason this specific note is worth opening?

Use high contrast against the real image region. A bright yellow accent is common and can attract attention, but switch colors whenever it loses contrast or clashes with the brand.

## Layout patterns

- Sandwich: headline / face or object / payoff.
- Information center: large message integrated across the composition.
- Top/bottom: primary line above, secondary proof below.
- Four-corner: short fragments around a clean central image; reserve for images with strong negative space.

## Series behavior

Keep a successful cover grammar recognizable across a series: stable type, crop logic, color cue, and information order. Test one major variable at a time instead of changing the entire style every post.
