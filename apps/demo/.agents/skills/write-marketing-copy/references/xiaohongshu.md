# Xiaohongshu copy reference

Use this reference only when Xiaohongshu is the target. Treat it as an editorial heuristic, not a guarantee about the platform algorithm.

## Discovery and search

- Write for both recommendation discovery and active search.
- Put the audience's natural search phrase in the title when it fits. Prefer “how to take better photos” language over specialist terminology such as “composition theory.”
- Mine the user's source, audience questions, comments, support requests, and reviews for real phrasing. Do not keyword-stuff.
- Let the title establish the topic and angle; let cover copy add relevance, benefit, or tension.

## Useful title frames

- Benefit: “A practical way to …”
- Pain: “If you keep getting stuck at …”
- Relief: “The lower-friction way I …”
- Recognition: “You are not bad at X; you may be missing Y.”
- Contrarian: “What looks like X may actually be Y.”
- Evidence: “What changed after …”

Keep the title faithful to the note. Avoid absolute claims unless the source genuinely proves them.

## Read-through

- Deliver the promised answer in the opening lines.
- Keep paragraphs short enough to scan on a phone.
- Use one useful idea per paragraph or carousel board.
- Prefer concrete scenes, comparisons, checklists, and examples over generic advice.
- Close with one context-appropriate action: save for later, share with a specific person, answer a real question, or try one step.

## Human voice

- Build voice from the user's own samples when available.
- Use conversational wording without manufacturing slang.
- Vary rhythm and remove generic AI transitions.
- Never invent a personal story to create “authenticity.”
